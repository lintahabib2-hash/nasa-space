
# Claims to Use / Claims to Avoid

> Owner: Member 1 (Coordination, Science, Pitch)
>
> Purpose: Keep project descriptions, demonstrations, slides and claims scientifically accurate.

## 1. Claims We Can Use

| Area | Approved wording |
|---|---|
| Project purpose | CREW VITALS is a prototype for monitoring astronaut health-related check-ins, personal trends and relevant spaceflight health risks. |
| NASA research | NASA Human Research Program research informs the health-risk categories and indicators used in the prototype. |
| Simulated data | The demonstration uses clearly labeled simulated crew records to show daily check-ins and personal trend monitoring. |
| NASA environmental data | The project can display historical NASA-measured environmental observations when the selected dataset has been successfully retrieved and verified. |
| Radiation context | NASA radiation instrument data can provide historical radiation-monitoring context when its source, instrument and measurement units are identified. |
| Personal trends | The prototype can compare entered or simulated measurements with an individual's available historical baseline. |
| Offline capability | The application is designed to support local check-ins and access to previously available information when internet connectivity is unavailable, subject to testing. |
| Transparency | The Evidence Drawer helps users identify the source and limitations of information shown by the application. |
| Decision support | The prototype provides informational trends and review-only guidance. It does not replace professional medical judgment. |

## 2. Claims We Must Avoid

| Avoid saying | Why it is not acceptable |
|---|---|
| The system is clinically validated for astronauts. | No clinical validation on astronaut populations has been established. |
| The AI predicts astronaut diseases or medical emergencies. | The prototype does not have validated clinical prediction targets. |
| Our simulated records are real NASA astronaut data. | Simulated records are fictional demonstration data. |
| NASA environmental measurements represent an astronaut's personal exposure. | Environmental sensors may measure a particular location or experiment hardware, not an individual's exposure. |
| Radiation readings directly predict cancer risk. | Instrument readings alone cannot establish individual biological risk. |
| Bed-rest participants are astronauts in space. | Bed-rest studies are ground-based analog research. |
| The system diagnoses depression, stress or cognitive impairment. | Self-reports and brief task measurements do not establish a medical diagnosis. |
| Exercise completion proves that bone or muscle loss has been prevented. | Exercise records alone cannot establish those biological outcomes. |
| The system provides real-time spacecraft medical monitoring. | Historical datasets and simulated records must not be presented as live spacecraft telemetry. |
| The application guarantees astronaut safety. | A prototype cannot guarantee medical or mission safety. |

## 3. Required Labels in the Application and Presentation

Use these labels wherever relevant:

- **NASA-measured:** A measurement retrieved from an identified NASA dataset and verified.
- **Analog-measured:** A measurement from a ground-based analog study, such as bed-rest research.
- **External Earth reference:** Data from an Earth-based external research dataset.
- **Simulated:** Fictional data created for demonstration and testing.
- **Manually entered:** Information entered by a user.

Never remove these labels when displaying, exporting or discussing measurements.

## 4. Approved Presentation Statement

CREW VITALS is a research-informed astronaut health-monitoring prototype. It combines health check-ins, explainable personal trends, NASA research-based risk information and clearly identified data sources. Its demonstration uses simulated crew records and may display separate historical NASA environmental observations. It is designed to support awareness and review, not to diagnose medical conditions or predict clinical outcomes.

## 5. Final Review Rules

1. Every scientific claim must have an identifiable supporting source.
2. Clearly distinguish NASA-measured, analog, external, simulated and manually entered information.
3. Do not combine unrelated people, missions, populations or locations on timestamp alone.
4. Do not invent missing measurements, clinical thresholds or NASA findings.
5. Do not describe historical observations as real-time data.
6. Do not use diagnostic, treatment or guaranteed-safety language.
7. If a source or measurement has not been verified, disclose that limitation.
8. Member 1 must review scientific claims before they are used in final presentation material.
