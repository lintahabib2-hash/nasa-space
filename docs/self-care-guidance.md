
# Self-Care Guidance

> Owner: Member 1 (Coordination, Science, Pitch)
>
> Purpose: Provide general, non-medical, review-only suggestions that support astronaut awareness and healthy daily routines.
>
> Important: This guidance is informational. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment or an emergency-response procedure.

## 1. Sleep and Circadian Routine

**When relevant:** A person reports reduced sleep, irregular sleep timing or poor subjective sleep quality.

Suggested guidance:

- Try to follow the sleep and wake schedule established by the mission team.
- Keep the sleep environment as comfortable and quiet as mission conditions allow.
- Reduce unnecessary light and distractions during the planned sleep period when possible.
- Record sleep duration and subjective sleep quality consistently.
- Discuss persistent sleep difficulties with the appropriate mission medical team.

**Avoid claiming:** That a particular number of sleep hours automatically indicates medical impairment.

## 2. Physical Activity and Exercise

**When relevant:** Activity or exercise records show a change from the person's usual routine.

Suggested guidance:

- Follow the exercise plan approved by the mission team.
- Record completed exercise sessions and activity duration accurately.
- Allow appropriate recovery according to the mission schedule.
- Report persistent discomfort, unusual fatigue or difficulty completing planned activities to the mission medical team.

**Avoid claiming:** That exercise completion alone proves prevention of bone or muscle loss.

## 3. Hydration and Daily Routine

**When relevant:** A person reports difficulty maintaining their usual daily routine.

Suggested guidance:

- Follow the mission team's hydration and nutrition instructions.
- Record relevant daily check-ins consistently.
- Maintain regular routines where mission conditions permit.
- Report persistent concerns or unusual symptoms through the appropriate medical communication process.

**Avoid claiming:** That the prototype can determine dehydration or nutritional deficiencies without validated measurements.

## 4. Stress and Behavioral Well-being

**When relevant:** A person voluntarily reports increased stress, low mood or difficulty maintaining routine.

Suggested guidance:

- Consider a short personal check-in to reflect on mood and well-being.
- Maintain appropriate communication with crewmates and mission support teams.
- Use relaxation or recreational activities approved by the mission team.
- Discuss persistent distress with qualified mission behavioral-health professionals.
- Respect personal privacy and voluntary participation in check-ins.

**Avoid claiming:** That mood entries, voice recordings or task scores can diagnose depression, anxiety or other mental-health conditions.

## 5. Cognitive Check-ins

**When relevant:** A person notices changes in concentration or performance on an optional brief task.

Suggested guidance:

- Complete optional tasks under consistent conditions when possible.
- Record interruptions, fatigue and other relevant context.
- Consider whether sleep, workload or environmental conditions may have changed.
- Discuss persistent concerns with the appropriate mission support team.

**Avoid claiming:** That a single task score establishes cognitive impairment or predicts mission failure.

## 6. Environmental Awareness

**When relevant:** Historical or currently available environmental measurements show a change.

Suggested guidance:

- Review the measurement source, location, timestamp and unit.
- Follow official mission environmental procedures.
- Report environmental concerns through established mission channels.
- Do not independently change equipment settings based on prototype information.

**Avoid claiming:** That a sensor reading from experiment hardware necessarily represents an astronaut's personal exposure.

## 7. Radiation Awareness

**When relevant:** Radiation instrument observations are displayed.

Suggested guidance:

- Review the instrument name, measurement unit, timestamp and data source.
- Follow official mission radiation-safety procedures.
- Refer radiation-related concerns to qualified mission personnel.

**Avoid claiming:** That raw instrument readings directly calculate an individual's cancer risk or determine whether shelter is required.

## 8. General Safety and Escalation

- The prototype must not replace mission medical procedures.
- Urgent symptoms or safety concerns must be handled through established mission emergency and medical communication protocols.
- Do not delay professional assistance while using the application.
- Missing, incomplete or uncertain data must be clearly identified.
- Users must be able to distinguish personal entries from external measurements and simulated examples.

## 9. Application Display Requirements

When guidance is displayed, the application should:

1. Use calm, respectful and non-alarming language.
2. Explain why a suggestion is being shown.
3. Identify the relevant measurement or check-in when available.
4. State that the suggestion is informational and review-only.
5. Provide a reminder to follow official mission procedures.
6. Avoid medical diagnoses, medication recommendations and treatment instructions.
